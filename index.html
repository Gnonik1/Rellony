Agent vs Evidence

The listing says it.
The record may not.

Every finding keeps three things apart: what a record actually says, what that could plausibly mean, and what you should go and ask.

Example finding — illustration of our report format

Claim from the listing

Extraction planning granted, suitable for full commercial kitchen.

Checking the record Unverified

Evidence

No planning record granting extraction at this address was found in the sources searched. The address appears only in unrelated neighbour notification records, the most recent 18/04417/NEI, dated 09 Nov 2018.

Interpretation

This does not establish that no permission exists — a consent can predate the searchable record, or sit under a different address format. It does mean the claim is currently unsupported by any record we could find.

Action

Ask the landlord or agent for the planning reference and the decision notice in writing, before the extraction claim influences what you are willing to pay.

Confirmed by a primary record No independent record found Record contradicts the claim

An agent’s own claim never counts as confirmation of itself. Where we cannot confirm something, we say so plainly rather than filling the gap.

What a report is not

  • It is not legal, planning, licensing or surveying advice, and it is not a substitute for a solicitor or a professional survey.
  • It does not guarantee that a property is suitable, or that permission will be granted for what you intend to do there.
  • It reflects the public record as it stood when the report was prepared. Records change, and gaps in them are reported as gaps.
  • Early access: reports are currently prepared by a person working through our evidence-checking process, not by an automated system.
