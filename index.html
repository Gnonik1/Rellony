Agent vs Evidence

The listing says it.
The record may not.

Every finding keeps three things apart: what a record actually says, what that could plausibly mean, and what you should go and ask.

Example finding — from our fictional sample report

Claim from the listing

Fully licensed restaurant, alcohol licence in place.

Checking the record Contradicted

Evidence

The council’s licensing register lists premises licence FIC/24/01234/PREM, granted 14 Jun 2024 to a company. Companies House shows that company was dissolved on 26 May 2026. No transfer or interim authority notice appears on the register since.

Interpretation

A company’s premises licence lapses when the company is dissolved, unless within 28 days someone applies to transfer it or gives an interim authority notice. None appears on the register, so unless one was made and not yet published, the licence has lapsed, even though the register still shows it as current.

Action

Ask the agent for proof of a transfer or interim authority notice before you agree the premium. If there is none, plan for a new premises licence before opening with alcohol.

What a report is not

  • It is not legal, planning, licensing or surveying advice, and it is not a substitute for a solicitor or a professional survey.
  • It does not guarantee that a property is suitable, or that permission will be granted for what you intend to do there.
  • It reflects the public record as it stood when the report was prepared. Records change, and gaps in them are reported as gaps.
